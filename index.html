The governance premium: what the report quantifies, what the ops side lives draft
Deloitte's 2026 Sports Outlook names 'the governance premium.' I spent years on the inside of that premium — and on the other side of it.
Deloitte's 2026 Sports Industry Outlook uses the phrase "governance premium."

It means institutional capital pays more for sports assets with clean governance structures. Analysts are starting to quantify what operators have known for years.

I was at FC Porto at board level. I ran operations at Liga Portugal. The governance premium is not a discount rate. It is not a due diligence checkbox.

It is a Monday morning call about a decision that was never documented. A compliance process that runs on institutional memory, not protocol. A committee that was not briefed on what the committee above it decided.

The premium exists because the gap between governance design and governance in practice is exactly where value leaks — and exactly where investors get surprised.

Deloitte can now put a number on it. The question no report answers: who inside the asset is accountable for closing that gap?

That person is rarely in the investment committee.

Alan Pace backed an academic sports management center. Read it as a governance play. draft
Alan Pace owns Espanyol and Burnley. He just backed a sports management center at IESE. This is not philanthropy.
Alan Pace owns Espanyol in La Liga and Burnley in the Premier League. He has now backed the IESE Sports Management Center.

Read it as a governance move, not a corporate donation.

Multi-club ownership is under regulatory scrutiny — from UEFA, from national federations, from fan coalitions. The institutional investor who owns your club has a legitimacy problem that a balance sheet does not solve.

Backing an academic center at a school like IESE puts PE in the room where the next cohort of sport directors is being trained. It shapes what "professional governance" looks like before regulators get to define it.

I ran operations inside a top European club at board level. The relationship between the capital that owns the asset and the operations team that runs it is the governance question nobody in a boardroom names directly.

Pace is naming it — quietly, in the right building.

Whether that produces better-run clubs or better-framed ones is the more interesting question.

NFL Minority Ownership: Infrastructure, Not Fandom draft
When media executives buy NFL minority stakes, the story isn't the stake — it's the queue position for when control becomes available.
In May, the NFL approved Ari Emanuel (1.4%) and Mark Shapiro (0.6%) as minority owners of the Las Vegas Raiders. The headline: TKO Group executives buying into an NFL franchise.

The real story is two levels deeper.

Silver Lake's Egon Durban doubled his stake from 11% to 22%. He now holds the right of first refusal on Mark Davis's controlling stake. Arctos Partners — recently acquired by KKR for $1.4 billion — simultaneously holds positions in three NFL franchises: Buffalo Bills, Los Angeles Chargers, and now the Cleveland Browns.

This is not sports fandom. This is infrastructure positioning.

The 2024 NFL rule change that permitted institutional minority investment didn't open a door — it opened a pipeline. The minority stake round at a $9.9B Raiders valuation isn't about influence today. It's about queue position for the moment control becomes available.

Governance doesn't move fast. Until it does.

Ramos, Sevilla, and the Execution Gap draft
Sergio Ramos had €444 million in financing agreements, a signed letter of intent, and 85% of Sevilla within reach. He also had four months before it all fell apart.
Sergio Ramos signed a letter of intent in January to acquire 85% of Sevilla FC for approximately €444 million. By May 27, Five Eleven Capital and all original co-investors had exited the deal. Ramos arrived at the negotiating table with a single new investor and a restructured proposal: €100 million for an initial 18% stake, followed by a €120 million capital increase.

Three days before the exclusivity deadline, the deal collapsed.

Sevilla's shareholders published a statement describing it as "months of deception" and "a premeditated scam." A €500,000 penalty clause was invoked.

The story being told is about one man's betrayal of a club he grew up in. That's not the interesting part.

The interesting part is structural: sporting authority and ownership execution are different skill sets. Completely different. A player who controlled a match for 20 years does not, by default, control a financing stack. The gap between those two things is exactly where deals fall apart — regardless of how many Champions Leagues someone won.

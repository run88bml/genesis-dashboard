England's Independent Football Regulator Goes Live draft
116 clubs. One new regulator. A licensing deadline most of them are only beginning to process.
The Independent Football Regulator went live for the 2026-27 season. 116 clubs across five tiers of English men's professional football, each needing a provisional operating licence before they can compete in 2027-28. The application window opens 1 November 2026 and closes 26 February 2027.

The IFR is modelled on the Financial Conduct Authority — a financial regulator's logic applied to an industry that self-governed for decades. The trigger was visible long before the Act: Bury FC, Derby County, Wigan Athletic. Clubs that collapsed while the structures meant to catch them didn't.

Two things went live immediately. The ODSE regime — Owners, Directors and Senior Executives — means no one takes ownership or a Senior Management Function at a regulated club without suitability clearance: source of funds, competence, integrity. And the financial framework changes: PSR is replaced by Squad Cost Ratio and a Sustainability and Systemic Resilience framework, now aligned to UEFA standards.

I spent years running operations in a professional league while regulators were rewriting the rules around us. The framework is never the hard part. What the IFR finds inside those 116 clubs when it actually looks — that's the real story.

Black Knight Completes First American Takeover of a Premiership Rugby Club draft
Exeter Chiefs sold. First full American takeover in Premiership Rugby. The deal structure is straightforward. What follows isn't.
The deal closed 30 June. Bill Foley's Black Knight — through Cannae Holdings — completed the first 100% American ownership of a Premiership Rugby club. Tony Rowe CBE stays on as CEO. The capital wipes the club's remaining debt. The stated plan for next season: spend to the Premiership salary cap.

That's the structure on day one. Foley is a documented multi-sport investor whose background is American franchises. The franchise model he knows is built on stable rosters, fixed geography, and controlled revenue distribution. Premiership Rugby runs on promotion, relegation, a hard salary cap, and club identities built over generations of community rugby in English counties.

The capital does what capital does: it clears the balance sheet and funds recruitment. The harder question is what happens at month 12, month 24 — when the assumptions imported from the American sports market meet the operational reality of an English rugby club.

American money has arrived in Premiership Rugby. The interesting part is not the press release.

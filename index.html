MLS owners prepare to vote on league's first new commissioner in 27 years draft
The vote on MLS's first new commissioner in 27 years is happening this week. The headline is who wins. The story is what the choice reveals.
The vote on MLS's first new commissioner in 27 years is happening this week. The headline is who wins. The story is what the choice reveals.

Two finalists, two operating theses.

Larry Berg spent 30 years at Apollo Global Management. He has been an LAFC owner since the club's first season. He chairs MLS's sporting committee. His lens: ownership, capital, internal governance.

David Nathanson ran Fox Soccer for five years and led the network's bids for the 2018, 2022, and 2026 World Cups. His lens: broadcast rights, media distribution, reach.

A vote for Berg says the league's next problem is governance and capital allocation. A vote for Nathanson says it is visibility and media.

Inside a league, this distinction is not abstract. The commissioner's office sets the operational priorities for everything below it — events, partnerships, broadcast, labour negotiations. The choice made this week reaches the ground before it appears in any strategy document.

Two-thirds of 30 owners need to agree on the answer. That alignment process is the real story.

UK FCA warns Premier League clubs over unlicensed crypto sponsorships draft
14 of 20 Premier League clubs carry a crypto or blockchain sponsor this season. In June, the FCA wrote to all of them.
14 of 20 Premier League clubs carry a crypto or blockchain sponsor this season. In June, the FCA wrote to all of them.

The warning: several of your commercial partners are not authorised to offer financial services in the UK. By associating their logo with your badge, you may be facilitating illegal financial promotion.

Chelsea are sponsored by BingX. Manchester City’s sleeve is OKX. Neither is on the FCA authorisation register.

The backdrop: from 2026-27, front-of-shirt gambling sponsors are banned. That removed roughly £80 million from Premier League commercial revenue. Crypto firms moved in fast to fill the gap.

Commercial teams at clubs have one mandate when that kind of money is on the table: close the deal. The FCA register check is supposed to be someone else’s job.

Across 14 clubs, that handoff did not happen.

This is not a crypto story. It is a governance story: commercial velocity outpacing regulatory due diligence. It happens every time a new sponsor category emerges faster than the compliance framework can track it.
